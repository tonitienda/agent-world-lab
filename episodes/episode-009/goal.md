# Forest to Market

Build a working timber supply chain in OpenTTD. A forest produces wood on one bank
of a river. A sawmill on the other bank converts delivered wood into goods. A town
near the sawmill accepts those goods. There is no road connecting the three sites
and no bridge across the river. You start with 40,000 cash and an existing 40,000 loan.

Your targets, checked together at the finish:

- Deliver at least **120 tonnes of wood to the designated sawmill**.
- Deliver at least **28 crates of goods from that sawmill to the designated town**.
- Complete the chain within **240 economy days**.
- Finish with at least **8,000 cash** and positive combined truck operating profit.
- Never borrow above the starting 40,000 loan; operate at most **six trucks**.

Choose the crossing location, road alignment, stops, depot, truck types, fleet split
and expansion order. The sawmill starts without goods; moving wood is a prerequisite
for the second leg. Buying more goods trucks cannot fix a shortage of incoming wood.
Waiting cargo, picked-up cargo and delivered cargo are separate measurements.
Passenger or mail income does not count toward either freight target.

The river is navigable terrain, not a decorative overlay. Bridges, roads, vehicles,
running costs and interest use the game's actual rules and prices. You may use more
than one crossing if you can afford it. No bridge is built for you. Town streets do
not provide a ready-made inter-site network.

Inspect production, cargo acceptance, crossing costs, distances and finances before
committing the budget. Watch both legs once they operate. Reallocate or add trucks
when measured cargo queues and deliveries justify it; explain the trade-off in your
public narration. It is legitimate to finish early or acknowledge failure.

Use concise public commentary for viewers: the route plan, first capital commitment,
first wood delivery, first goods production, a diagnosed bottleneck, any correction,
and the verified result. Keep coordinates and calculations in technical summaries.
Attach an explicit visual subject (stop, truck, industry or crossing) where supported
so the camera can follow the subject you are discussing.
